How to run one
  1. Tap Play it out. The Lichess board opens with the position loaded, oriented your way, you to move.
  2. Board menu (☰ under the board) → Continue from here → Play with the computer.
  3. Set the level listed and convert it.
  4. Won it clearly twice? Tick it off. Lost it twice? Tap Review the real game — you'll usually find you played your original move again.

Put a clock on it — 5+0, same as your real games. Your conversion problem and your clock problem are the same problem, and an untimed drill trains only half of it.

Level 3 (≈1400) is your mirror. Level 4 (≈1700) is the stretch. Levels are set per position — don't go higher; you'd lose for reasons that have nothing to do with technique.

The P groups are not played out. They're fifteen-second scans — read the note at the top of the priority set. The steps above apply to groups C/B/A/D only.

Order is P → C → B → A → D, top to bottom. Group D last, and read its note first.

Priority — hanging material

The single biggest source of thrown-away wins, and the reason this set sits ahead of the endgame drills. Across 1,515 blitz games there are 239 middlegame moves you played while already winning by +1.5 or more, where a capture worth at least a pawn and a half was sitting on the board and you played something else. 184 of them were threats your opponent already had; 55 you created yourself. Ranked worst-first by how much of the win each one cost.

Run these differently. Don't play them out against the computer — that trains the wrong muscle. Open the position, give yourself fifteen seconds, and say out loud what your opponent takes if you do nothing. Then open the spoiler. The skill is the scan, not the calculation, and fifteen seconds is roughly what you'll have in a real 5+0 game.
